Better results

The prompt recipe

A prompt is the words you type to Claude. You get better results when you tell it these five things, the way you would brief a new colleague.

  1. RRoleWho should it act as? For example, "an experienced math professor."
  2. CContextWhat does it need to know?
  3. AAudienceWho will read the result?
  4. TToneHow should it sound?
  5. CConstraintsLength, format, and limits.

Choosing a tool

Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, or Copilot?

They are more alike than different. Here is what is going on under the hood, where they differ, and which fits your subject. Checked October 15, 2026. These tools change every few months, so model names especially will go out of date.

How they all work

The same core technology

Each is a large language model: trained on huge amounts of text to predict the next word. That is why it writes so smoothly, and why it can sound sure and still be wrong.

Bigger and smaller models

Each company has a strongest model (slower, more careful) and faster, smaller ones. Free plans mostly get the faster ones. Paying mainly buys the strongest model and higher limits.

Thinking mode

The model works step by step before it answers. Slower, but better for math, logic, and grading. All four have one.

What it knows

It learned from text up to a cutoff date. For anything recent it searches the web, and then it is only as good as the pages it finds. Open its sources.

Working memory

Each chat holds a limited amount of text (the context window). In very long chats or huge files, early parts can get lost. Start a New chat for each new task.

Your privacy setting

Personal accounts may use your chats to improve the AI unless you turn that off. School and work versions usually do not. That is why student work goes only into a tool CAU approves.

Which fits your subject?

  • Math, statistics, physics, chemistry, engineeringAny of the four. Turn on thinking mode, and add "Use code to check the arithmetic." It predicts text, so numbers can slip unless it calculates. Check every step.
  • Writing-heavy courses: English, history, education, social workAny of the four. Paste the same prompt into two of them and keep the voice you like better.
  • Research and current eventsUse web search or a research report: Gemini Deep Research (free), ChatGPT deep research, or Claude Research (paid). Open every source. AI sometimes cites pages that don't say what it claims.
  • Long documents: accreditation reports, grants, book chaptersPaid versions read far more at once. On a free plan, split a long file into parts.
  • Art, design, and pictures for slidesChatGPT, Gemini, or Copilot make pictures. Claude makes charts and diagrams, not pictures. Check scientific images closely; labels often come out wrong.
  • Email, meeting notes, Office filesCopilot lives in Outlook and Teams. Gemini lives in Gmail and Google Docs.
  • Anything about students: names, grades, their workOnly a tool CAU approves. CAU's sign-in runs through Microsoft, so Copilot Chat with your CAU login may be the protected option. Ask IT to confirm before you use it with student information.

Print the one-page version

The bottom line: for most teaching tasks, the four are closer than the ads suggest. The best one is the one you will actually use, with the privacy setting you want. Pick one and learn it well. When the stakes are high, ask a second one the same question.

Use it well

Four habits to keep

Protect privacy

Leave out student names, grades, and ID numbers. Real student work goes only into a tool CAU approves. FERPA still applies.

Check the facts

AI can sound sure and still be wrong. Verify dates, sources, and numbers.

Stay the author

Treat every result as a first draft. Edit it until it sounds like you.

Be open about it

Follow CAU guidance and your course policy on AI use.
